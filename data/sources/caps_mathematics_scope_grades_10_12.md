## Purpose of Mathematics in CAPS

Mathematics in the FET Phase (Grades 10-12) develops abstract thinking,
problem-solving, mathematical reasoning, and the ability to communicate
mathematical arguments. It provides essential grounding for further study
in engineering, science, commerce, and financial disciplines. The
National Senior Certificate (NSC) mathematics examination consists of
two three-hour papers (Paper 1 and Paper 2) each worth 150 marks.


## Paper 1 vs Paper 2 breakdown

Paper 1 typically covers:
- Algebra and Equations (30 marks in Grade 12)
- Number Patterns, Sequences, and Series (25 marks in Grade 12)
- Functions and Graphs (30 marks in Grade 12)
- Financial Mathematics (15 marks in Grade 12)
- Differential Calculus (35 marks in Grade 12)
- Counting Principle and Probability (15 marks in Grade 12)

Paper 2 typically covers:
- Statistics (20 marks in Grade 12)
- Analytical Geometry (40 marks in Grade 12)
- Trigonometry (50 marks in Grade 12)
- Euclidean Geometry and Measurement (40 marks in Grade 12)


## Grade 10 topic scope (paraphrased)

### Algebra
Learners work with algebraic expressions including factorisation
(common factor, difference of squares, trinomials of the form
ax² + bx + c). Simplification of rational expressions. Solving linear
equations with brackets and fractions on both sides. Solving quadratic
equations by factorisation. Solving simultaneous linear equations by
substitution and by elimination. Applying laws of exponents including
zero and negative exponents.

### Functions and Graphs
Linear functions y = ax + q. Quadratic functions in the form
y = ax² + q and later y = a(x + p)² + q. Hyperbolic functions
y = a/x + q. Exponential functions y = a·b^x + q where b > 0 and
b ≠ 1. For each function type: intercepts with the axes, axes of
symmetry, turning points, asymptotes, effect of parameters a, p, q.

### Number Patterns
Linear number patterns with a constant first difference. Deriving
the general term T_n. Recognising and continuing patterns from a
list of terms.

### Analytical Geometry
Distance between two points using the distance formula
d = √((x₂ - x₁)² + (y₂ - y₁)²). Gradient of a line joining two
points m = (y₂ - y₁)/(x₂ - x₁). Midpoint of a line segment.

### Trigonometry
Definitions of sin, cos, tan in a right-angled triangle: sin θ =
opposite/hypotenuse, cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse, tan θ = opposite
/adjacent (SOHCAHTOA). Special angles 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°. Solving
right-angled triangles. Simple problems involving heights, distances,
and angles of elevation and depression.

### Euclidean Geometry
Investigating properties of quadrilaterals and other polygons.
Parallel-line angle relationships. Introductory similarity of
triangles.

### Financial Mathematics
Simple interest: A = P(1 + rn). Compound interest: A = P(1 + r)^n.
Hire purchase agreements. Exchange rates and inflation.

### Statistics
Measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode) for ungrouped
data. Range and five-number summary. Box-and-whisker plots.

### Probability
Terminology: outcome, event, probability. Union and intersection of
events. Mutually exclusive events. Complementary events. Venn
diagrams. P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A ∩ B).


## Grade 11 topic scope (paraphrased)

### Algebra
Solving quadratic equations by:
- factorisation
- completing the square
- the quadratic formula: x = (-b ± √(b² - 4ac)) / (2a)
The discriminant Δ = b² - 4ac determines the nature of roots:
Δ > 0 real distinct roots, Δ = 0 real equal roots, Δ < 0 non-real
roots. Solving quadratic inequalities by finding critical values and
analysing signs. Solving simultaneous equations where one is linear
and one is quadratic. Working with surds including rationalisation
of monomial denominators.

### Functions and Graphs
Effect of parameters on the graphs of quadratic, hyperbolic and
exponential functions. Inverse of a linear function. Transformations
including reflections about the axes and shifts.

### Number Patterns
Quadratic number patterns with a constant SECOND difference. Deriving
T_n = an² + bn + c for a quadratic sequence.

### Analytical Geometry
Equation of a line in the form y = mx + c and y - y₁ = m(x - x₁).
Angle of inclination θ where tan θ = m. Parallel lines have equal
gradients; perpendicular lines satisfy m₁ · m₂ = -1.

### Trigonometry
Identities: the quotient identity tan θ = sin θ/cos θ; the square
identity sin²θ + cos²θ = 1. Compound angle formulae including
sin(A + B) = sinA·cosB + cosA·sinB and cos(A + B) = cosA·cosB -
sinA·sinB. Reduction formulae in each quadrant. Co-function
identities: sin(90° - θ) = cos θ. Solving trigonometric equations
including general solutions. Sine, cosine, and area rules for
non-right-angled triangles.

### Euclidean Geometry
Circle theorems: the tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the
radius at the point of contact. Inscribed angles subtended by the
same arc are equal. The angle at the centre is twice the angle at
the circumference for the same arc. Cyclic quadrilateral properties.

### Financial Mathematics
Future and present value of annuities. F = x·[(1 + i)^n - 1]/i.
P = x·[1 - (1 + i)^(-n)]/i. Effective versus nominal interest rates
and the relationship between them.

### Statistics
Variance and standard deviation for grouped and ungrouped data.
Symmetric versus skewed distributions. Scatter plots and the least-
squares regression line.

### Probability
Independent versus dependent events. Tree diagrams. The fundamental
counting principle for sequential choices.


## Grade 12 topic scope (paraphrased)

### Algebra (Sequences and Series)
Arithmetic sequences: T_n = a + (n - 1)d. Sum S_n = n/2·[2a + (n - 1)d].
Geometric sequences: T_n = a·r^(n - 1). Sum S_n = a(r^n - 1)/(r - 1)
for r ≠ 1. Sum to infinity for a convergent geometric series
(|r| < 1): S_∞ = a/(1 - r). Sigma notation.

### Functions
Formal definition of a function. Inverse functions. Logarithmic
function y = log_b(x) as the inverse of the exponential
y = b^x. Log laws: log(xy) = log x + log y; log(x/y) = log x - log y;
log(x^n) = n·log x.

### Differential Calculus
Limits computed by direct substitution and by factor-then-simplify.
Differentiation from first principles: f'(x) = lim (h→0)
[f(x + h) - f(x)]/h. Rules of differentiation: power rule, sum rule,
chain rule, product rule, quotient rule. Sketching cubic functions
using the first and second derivatives to find turning points and
points of inflection. Real-world optimisation problems (maximise or
minimise a quantity subject to constraints).

### Analytical Geometry
Equation of a circle in centre-radius form (x - a)² + (y - b)² = r².
Finding the equation of a tangent to a circle at a given point of
tangency, using the fact that the tangent is perpendicular to the
radius at that point.

### Trigonometry
Double-angle formulae: sin 2A = 2·sinA·cosA; cos 2A = cos²A - sin²A =
1 - 2sin²A = 2cos²A - 1. Applications in two and three dimensions
using the sine, cosine, and area rules. Angles of elevation and
depression in 3D geometric problems.

### Euclidean Geometry
The proportionality theorem. Similar triangles (AA, SSS, SAS
similarity). Ratio and proportion. The midpoint theorem.

### Financial Mathematics
Future value and present value of ordinary annuities. Sinking-fund
payments. Home loans and amortisation. Effect of partial payments.

### Statistics
Ogive (cumulative frequency curve). Standard deviation for grouped
data. Symmetry and skewness. Interpretation of statistical outputs.

### Probability
Fundamental counting principle. Factorial notation n!. Number of
permutations of n distinct objects: n!. Number of arrangements when
some objects are identical: n!/(a!·b!·...) for repeated groups.


## NSC assessment style — what markers look for

Every solution should show the METHOD used, the WORKING done, and the
FINAL ANSWER. Mark codes commonly used in memos:

- (M) method mark — awarded for choosing a correct approach
- (A) accuracy mark — awarded for a correct value
- (CA) consistent-answer mark — awarded when the final answer follows
  correctly from prior work even if that work had an earlier slip
- (S) substitution mark — awarded for correctly substituting values
- (R) reason mark — Euclidean geometry: awarded for citing the theorem

Answers should be in exact form (surds, fractions, π, e) unless the
question explicitly asks for a decimal, in which case round to 2
decimal places by default. Trigonometric ratios use the notation
sin θ, cos θ, tan θ without parentheses (only compound arguments like
sin(A + B) use them). Money is expressed in Rands (R100 or R100,00),
distances in kilometres or metres, temperature in degrees Celsius.
